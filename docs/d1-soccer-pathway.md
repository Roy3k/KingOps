# The Bellevue College → D1 Soccer Pathway

*A two-part plan: (1) how to support our son's move from Bellevue College (NWAC) to a Division 1 program by his third year, and (2) the founder-lens view — what a Pieter Levels or Garry Tan would build for everyone else walking this same path.*

*Drafted July 2026. Facts below reflect the post-House-settlement, post-June-2026 rule landscape and should be re-verified each season — this space is changing fast.*

---

## Why this moment is unusual (the "why now" that anchors both parts)

Three structural changes hit the JUCO→D1 pathway in the last 18 months, and they all point the same direction:

1. **The House settlement repriced college soccer.** Starting 2025-26, D1 men's soccer moved from 9.9 scholarship equivalencies to a 28-player roster cap where *every* roster spot can be a full scholarship (at the school's discretion). Walk-on spots are disappearing; every roster spot is now an explicit investment decision. Coaches want proven, physically mature, immediately eligible players — which is exactly what a good two-year-college sophomore is.

2. **The June 2026 NCAA rule change removed the JUCO transfer penalty.** The D1 Board adopted legislation aligning two-year-college transfers with the four-year transfer framework: leave your school academically eligible, meet D1 progress-toward-degree at the new school, and you're immediately eligible. The old maze of qualifier/non-qualifier distinctions is largely gone.

3. **Eligibility clocks are loosening.** The NCAA approved a five-seasons-in-five-years model (phasing in for 2027-28 enrollees, with transition options), and the Pavia litigation is actively challenging whether JUCO seasons should count against NCAA eligibility at all. **Planning assumption for us: his two NWAC seasons count as two of four NCAA seasons, leaving two D1 years. Anything the courts or NCAA add later is upside, not the plan.**

Net effect: the two-year college is no longer a detour — it's becoming a legitimate development league for D1, at the exact moment D1 rosters got smaller and more selective. That tension (more demand for transfers, fewer total spots) is the whole story, for our son and for any product built here.

# Part 1 — The Player Plan

## The honest baseline

- **NWAC context.** Bellevue plays in the Northwest Athletic Conference — 37 member colleges, the largest community-college conference in the country. NWAC players who "move up" most commonly land at **D2 and NAIA** programs (recent Bellevue examples: Western Oregon, Northwest Nazarene — both D2). Direct NWAC→D1 happens, but it's the standout-player outcome, not the median.
- **The local D1 ladder is dense but brutal.** Within driving distance: **UW (Big Ten — reigning national champions), Seattle U (WCC), Portland, Oregon State, Gonzaga.** That's an unusual concentration of D1 soccer for one region, plus a deep D2/NAIA bench (Western Washington, Seattle Pacific, Saint Martin's) and the Sounders/MLS-adjacent ecosystem raising the region's profile. Great for exposure; also means D1 local spots draw national and international competition.
- **Therefore the real goal:** *the best four-year offer where he actually plays*, with D1 as the stretch target. The strongest path to D1 sometimes runs NWAC → strong D2 → D1 portal. Framing it this way keeps year-two decisions rational instead of ego-driven.

## The one thing that silently kills this plan: credits

D1 progress-toward-degree requires roughly **40% of a bachelor's degree completed entering year three**. Not "60 college credits" — 40% *of a specific degree at the receiving school*. A transcript full of credits that transfer but don't apply to his intended major makes him functionally ineligible even with great film.

**Action (this summer, before his first class):**
- Register with the **NCAA Eligibility Center** now.
- Pick an intended four-year major (even provisionally) and sit with a Bellevue advisor to build a two-year course map where **every course both transfers to and applies at** UW/Seattle U-type schools.
- Target the **AA degree completed by spring of year two** — it's still the cleanest signal, and NWAC's own second-season eligibility (36 credits, 2.0 GPA) falls out of it for free.
- GPA target is **3.3+, not the 2.0 minimum**. Coaches use GPA as a risk screen, and academic money stacks with athletic money — post-House, a player who's "cheap" academically is easier to fit on a roster.

## Three-year timeline

### Year 0 — Summer 2026 (now)
- Eligibility Center registration, credit map, major selection (above).
- **Film infrastructure**: confirm whether Bellevue films every match (Veo or similar). If not, we buy/run our own Veo or Trace setup (~$1,200/yr). Full-match film from game one of year one is non-negotiable — sophomore-year recruiting is built on freshman-year footage.
- Physical baseline: this is the year strength and speed development compounds. D1 coaches evaluating a JUCO transfer are asking "is he physically a D1 player *today*" — that's the entire premise of recruiting from two-year colleges.

### Year 1 — 2026-27 (freshman season)
- **On field:** earn a real role by conference play. Minutes > badge of the program.
- **Academics:** 36+ credits on the transfer map, 3.3+.
- **Build the target list (winter):** ~25 schools in three tiers — 6-8 D1 (stretch), 10-12 D2/strong NAIA (target), 5-6 safety. For each: roster page audit — *do they carry JUCO/transfer players? How many roster spots open in his position for fall 2028?* Post-House, roster math is public and it's the most honest signal of fit.
- **First outreach (spring):** a 3-minute highlight reel + full-match links + transcript + one-page profile, emailed by *him* (not us) to position coaches. NWAC players aren't in the NCAA transfer portal — that's an **advantage**: no windows, no compliance queue, direct contact any time.
- **Summer 2027:** high-level summer ball is the biggest exposure lever in the Northwest — USL League Two / NPSL / EPLWA sides (Ballard FC, Crossfire, etc.) are where D1 staffs actually watch players in this region. Add 2-3 **ID camps chosen strategically**: only at schools from his target list that actually take transfers, ideally where a staff member has already replied to an email. ID camps are evaluation-for-fee everywhere else.

### Year 2 — 2027-28 (the recruiting season)
- **On field:** All-NWAC-level performance is the credential that opens D1 doors.
- **Fall:** weekly film cut within 48 hours of each match; update every coach who's engaged. Coach relationships are maintained by cadence, not blasts.
- **Winter:** the Bellevue coaching staff is the multiplier — JUCO coaches place players every year and their word carries more weight with D1 staffs than any highlight reel. Make sure the head coach knows his target list and is making calls.
- **Spring:** visits, offers, decision. Verify at signing: progress-toward-degree audit at the receiving school, remaining eligibility under whatever the clock rules are by then, and what the scholarship actually covers (post-House "full scholarship possible" ≠ "full scholarship offered" — most programs still don't fully fund 28).

## Our role (the part I control)

Be the **ops department, not the agent**: run the film pipeline, maintain the coach-contact spreadsheet, fund the right camps, drive to the visits. Coaches recruit players who run their own recruitment; parents who front the relationship are a red flag. He sends every email.

**Budget line for KingOps (F2 allocation):** film setup ~$1,200/yr, ID camps ~$150-400 each × 3, summer club fees, visit travel → **~$3-5k/yr for two years.** For calibration: that's less than a single NCSA "full service" package ($2,500-5,000/yr), and it buys real things instead of a call center.

# Part 2 — The Founder Lens

*"Others on his path" is not a small group, and the incumbents serving them are bad. Here's the Levels take and the Tan take.*

## The market in one paragraph

Roughly 350+ two-year-college men's soccer programs (NJCAA ~230, CCCAA ~90, NWAC ~30+) ≈ **8-9k players at any moment**, nearly all of whom intend to move up, plus their parents, plus ~350 JUCO coaches whose recruiting pitch to next year's freshmen *is their placement record*, plus ~200 D1 and ~700 D2/NAIA programs that now — post-House — must fill capped rosters with proven, immediately eligible players. The demand side is desperate for **verified signal** (real film, real transcripts, real eligibility status) and the supply side is drowning in fragmented information. The incumbents (NCSA at $2,500-5,000/yr with opaque sales-call pricing, SportsRecruits sold to clubs, FieldLevel's network) all point at the high-school market and treat JUCO as an afterthought.

## The wedge insight

**JUCO players can't enter the NCAA transfer portal.** The portal is the infrastructure for four-year transfers; two-year players have *nothing* — they cold-email. Meanwhile the June 2026 rule change just made them immediately eligible, i.e., maximally valuable. There is a missing marketplace: **"the portal for players who aren't in the portal."** That's the venture-sized framing. Everything below ladders toward it.

## The Pieter Levels version (ship this month, charge from day one)

1. **Week 1 — Build in public.** Document our son's Bellevue→D1 campaign as a public playbook: the credit map, the outreach templates, the target-list spreadsheet, the film workflow, what each camp cost and what it returned. This is the exact Nomad List move: the founder is user #1, the content is the top of funnel, and nobody can fake the authenticity. (XtraMath taught us how education content compounds; same muscle.)
2. **Month 1 — The directory.** "Who moved up" — a scraped, structured database of every NWAC/NJCAA/CCCAA player who transferred to a four-year program (the conferences publish these lists; D1 roster pages show JUCO pedigree). Sort by: which D1 programs *actually* take two-year transfers, by sport, by position, by year. This data exists nowhere in one place and it's the first question every JUCO family asks. Free to browse, SEO gold.
3. **Quarter 1 — The toolkit ($99/yr per player).** Eligibility checklist, credit-transfer mapper ("will your Bellevue classes count at Seattle U in this major?" — the boring, unsexy, silent killer from Part 1 turned into software), outreach CRM, film-hosting profile. Charge players a Levels price, not an NCSA price. AI makes the credit-audit and film-tagging problems solvable by one founder now — that's new since 2024.
4. **Ongoing — Do things that don't scale.** Personally run the playbook for 5 of his Bellevue teammates for free. Their outcomes are the case studies; their coach becomes the first evangelist.

Revenue math sanity check: 8-9k players × modest 10% penetration × $99 ≈ $80-90k/yr — a healthy indie business by itself, and that's one sport and one gender before touching baseball (where JUCO→D1 is a firehose) or the program-side revenue.

## The Garry Tan version (build for a community you love; live in the future)

The venture-scale product is the **trust layer for the two-year transfer market**:

- **Verified athlete profiles** — film verified against full-match footage, transcripts and progress-toward-degree verified against registrar data, eligibility status computed, physical data from combine-style events. The reason coaches pay for nothing today is that nothing today is verified.
- **Sell to the demand side.** Players ride cheap or free (they're broke — pricing them like NCSA does is both immoral and bad strategy). Programs pay real SaaS money ($3-10k/yr) for a filterable pipeline of eligible, verified two-year transfers — because post-House, a roster-spot mistake costs a full scholarship's worth of value.
- **Distribution through JUCO coaches, not athletes.** 350 coaches whose careers depend on placement, each touching 25 players a year, each desperate to systematize what they now do by text message and favors. The coach is the channel, the athlete is the user, the four-year program is the customer. (Classic YC-shaped three-sided wedge that starts as a boring tool for one side.)
- **Sequencing:** NWAC men's soccer (37 schools, one tight community we're personally inside) → NJCAA/CCCAA soccer → JUCO baseball/softball/basketball → the general "non-portal transfer" layer, including NAIA→NCAA and international. Each conference is a beachhead with the same data model.
- **Why-now stack:** House roster caps (demand shock) + June 2026 eligibility alignment (friction collapse) + Pavia (if JUCO years stop counting, the two-year route becomes strictly *better* than a D1 bench year and supply explodes) + AI (verification and film analysis at solo-founder cost).
- **Founder-market fit:** a parent living the problem in real time, with an ed-tech operating background and school-distribution experience via XtraMath. Tan's test — "would you work on this for ten years even if it stayed small?" — is easy to pass when user #1 is your kid.

## The honest risks

- **Niche gravity:** JUCO families have less money than HS-recruiting families; player-paid revenue alone stays indie-sized. The venture case *requires* program-side revenue.
- **Rule volatility is a double-edged sword:** Pavia or the next NCAA vote could reshape the market annually. (It's also the moat for whoever becomes the trusted explainer — see step 1.)
- **Incumbent response:** FieldLevel is closest to the coach-network model. Differentiation is verification depth + two-year-college focus, not features.
- **Conflict-of-interest hygiene:** if this gets built while our son is being recruited, keep his recruitment and the product visibly separate.

## The next three concrete actions (both parts)

1. **This week:** NCAA Eligibility Center registration + book the Bellevue academic-advisor meeting with a draft major (Part 1's critical path).
2. **This month:** start the public playbook — one page: "The Bellevue College → D1 plan, published as we run it." Costs nothing, tests whether anyone cares, and is useful to his teammates on day one either way.
3. **By September:** scrape the 2025 and 2026 NWAC transfer lists + cross-reference D1 rosters for JUCO pedigree → the seed of the directory, and simultaneously the ground truth for how often this path actually works, which sharpens Part 1's target list.
